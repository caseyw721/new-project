# Off-the-shelf components and sourcing for a budget glass lathe (2025-2026 prices)

**How these notes were gathered and what the prices mean.** All prices are USD as reported on 2026-10-01. The research environment could reach web search but almost every vendor and forum domain (Vevor, Amazon, eBay, Alibaba, Shars, Grizzly, Harbor Freight, Little Machine Shop, Litton, StepperOnline, Teknic, Deublin distributors, Practical Machinist, Lampwork Etc, Hobby-Machinist, repairfaq.org, etc.) was blocked for direct page fetches. Prices and specs below are therefore the figures the search engine extracted from those vendor pages (each line links the page it came from), not figures read off the page by hand. Treat every number as "list price seen in a search snippet on 2026-10-01" and re-verify before purchase. Shipping is noted only where a source stated it. Where a figure could not be found at all it is listed under Gaps rather than estimated.

Target machine recap (from the assignment): 4-6 in self-centering chucks at both ends, hollow spindles with 1.5-2 in through-bore, 2-4 ft between chucks, synchronized rotation at ~5-200 rpm, air/vacuum through at least one spindle.

## Key question 1: Chucks (self-centering scroll chucks with large through-holes, mounting, weight, runout, price; soft jaws; purpose-made glass-lathe chucks; wood-lathe and micro-lathe chucks)

### Takeaway
Chinese K11 (3-jaw) / K12 (4-jaw self-centering) / K72 (4-jaw independent) scroll chucks are the only cheap option with a usable through-bore: a 160 mm (6 in) K11/K12 has a 45 mm (1.77 in) through-hole for about $60-140, and a 200 mm (8 in) K72 has a 70 mm (2.76 in) through-hole for about $100-280. Purpose-made glass-lathe scroll chucks (Litton) cost $1,785-2,700 each new, i.e. 15-30x the Chinese chucks, and a complete used Litton/Heathway lathe costs $6,000-25,000.

### Inferences
- For the 1.5-2 in through-bore target, 125 mm chucks (30 mm hole) are too small; 160 mm K11/K12 (45 mm = 1.77 in hole) meet the low end, and only the 200 mm K72 (70 mm hole) or a 200 mm K11 clears 2 in. Two 160 mm chucks cost roughly $120-200 total from Vevor/Made-in-China; two 200 mm K72s roughly $200-560.
- Plain-back K11/K12 chucks mount on a bolt circle with a register recess (Shars lists a 5.591 in bolt circle for 6 in), so an adapter plate (laser-cut or machined, see Key question 10) is needed to put them on a tube spindle; Shars' pre-threaded 1-1/2 x 8 and 2-1/4 x 8 back plates are the drop-in option for wood-lathe-thread spindles.
- The 0.003 in TIR Shars spec and the 2500-3600 rpm ratings are far beyond what a 5-200 rpm glass lathe needs; runout for glass work is dominated by jaw grip on tubing, which is why builders make extended/soft jaws rather than buying precision chucks.
- Soft jaws are available for K11 sizes from European and Australian stock; a US buyer can expect roughly $60-70 plus international shipping per set, or machine aluminum jaw extensions as the Lampwork Etc builder did.
- The Alibaba "glass lathe chuck" listings at $299-444 appear to be small complete lathe heads rather than bare chucks; nothing cheaper than the Litton scroll chucks was found as a purpose-made glass chuck sold separately.

### Gaps
- Could not verify through-hole, bolt-circle, weight and TIR for K11-125, K11-200, K12-125 or Vevor's 8 in 3-jaw directly (manufacturer spec pages blocked); only K11-160 (45 mm), K12-160 (45 mm), K72-200 (70 mm), Shars 6 in (1.772 in) and a 30 mm figure for K11-125 were captured.

### Inferences
- The cheapest route to a 1.5-2 in bore is a DOM tube (2.5 in OD x 2 in ID or 2 in OD x 1.5 in ID) running directly in two UCP/UCF self-aligning ball-bearing units of matching bore (2 in -> UCP211-32 class; 1-3/4 in -> UCP209-28), with a laser-cut or machined chuck flange welded/bolted to the tube end: two spindles' bearings about $65-160, tube about $20-60, flanges per Key question 10.
- Self-aligning UCP units tolerate the alignment errors of a hobby weldment, which matters more than their runout at 5-200 rpm; the trade-off is that the set-screw locking collars introduce a few thousandths of runout and the inserts are not preloaded like tapered rollers.
- Hollow-shaft steppers and NMRV030 gearboxes do not reach a 1.5 in bore, so "motor on the spindle" designs are limited to small-tube work; belt/chain drive to a separate tube spindle is the practical layout at this budget.
- A hollow-chuck welding positioner ($2,100-3,600) is a finished hollow spindle with chuck, bearings, worm drive and speed control, but at 0.5-20 rpm maximums (see Key question 8) and 100-250 lb class it is both too slow and far more expensive than two DIY spindles.

### Gaps
- No price was captured for UCP211-32 (2 in bore) or UCF208/UCF210 flange units, nor for tapered-roller bearings (e.g., 32010) — the searches that would have covered these did not run (budget exhausted).
- Speedy Metals' per-foot price for the 2.5 x 2.0 DOM could not be read; only the $/lb base price appeared.
- No off-the-shelf "headstock assembly" with a 1.5-2 in bore sold separately was found; Vevor's 1.1 kW 8x16 lathe advertises a 1.5 in spindle bore but as a whole machine ($1,220, Key question 8).

### Inferences
- For a glass lathe, the union sits at the outboard end of the headstock spindle and sees only a few psi of blow pressure and modest vacuum at <=200 rpm, so the 100 psi / 320 rpm generic union or the $219-272 DSTI SPS-5210 is adequate; the Deublin 17 series is the "buy once" option if the budget allows.
- The $1-20 swivel fittings are built for air lines, not sealed vacuum; they are the "budget tier" only if the design tolerates leakage (e.g., blowing only, or a mouth-blown hose).

### Inferences
- Torque reality check: at the chuck, a 12 Nm NEMA 34 stepper direct-driving a 6 in chuck is more than enough for glass (the DIY sources stress glass needs little torque); the reason to add a 3:1-5:1 belt reduction is to run the stepper at a smoother 15-1000 rpm instead of 5-200 rpm and to shrink the motor to a $50-80 NEMA 23 class.
- Two steppers/hybrid servos on one pulse train are "synchronous" only as long as neither stalls; closed-loop drivers (CL86T, ClearPath) report a fault instead of silently losing position, which is the main justification for the ~$30-150 premium over open-loop drivers.
- Two induction motors on two VFDs are the wrong tool: an induction motor's speed is set by slip, which varies with load and temperature, so two independently driven chucks will drift out of phase and twist the glass; nothing found in the sources priced this option because no builder uses it.
- Mechanical single-motor sync (one motor, a keyed or hex line shaft the length of the bed, a sliding coupler to the tailstock) is the cheapest electronics-free route (NMRV030 + pulleys + shaft ~$100-150) but the sliding spline/hex coupler and shaft support bearings are the parts not available off the shelf at 2-4 ft lengths without fabrication.
- A foot pedal for a stepper system is just a potentiometer in a pedal housing feeding the pulse-generator board's speed input; no priced pedal was captured (see Gaps).

- Mini-lathe headstocks (20 mm bore) match the Kube OpenLathe's 20 mm spindle class and are fine for tubing up to ~18 mm; they cannot meet the 1.5-2 in target.

- Used Atlas/Craftsman 12 in lathes ($800-1,000 with tooling) give a long cast bed and a 1-1/2 x 8 threaded nose that Shars' threaded-backplate chucks fit directly, but their spindle bore (about 0.75 in on these models, not verified here) is the limiting factor.

## Key question 10: Fabrication costs for the few custom parts (spindle, chuck adapter plates, tailstock base, laser-cut plate)

### Takeaway
Laser-cut steel plate from SendCutSend starts around $20-33 per small part at quantity one with a $39 minimum order, while one-off CNC-machined metal parts from online services run roughly $50-500 each (PCBWay quoted $258-334 for an aluminum block), so adapter plates and tailstock bases should be designed as flat laser-cut parts and only the spindle ends sent for turning.

### Cited Findings
- SendCutSend: laser-cut steel from $1/part, $39 minimum order; examples: mild steel 0.059 in, 2 x 2 in, $19.51 ea; cold-rolled steel 0.119 in, 2.5 x 4.75 in, $29.14 at qty 1 ($11.75 at qty 100); cold-rolled 0.059 in, 5 x 6 in, $32.48 at qty 1 ($7.99 at qty 100); volume discounts up to 80%; the site notes examples are estimates and uploading a file gives instant pricing — [SendCutSend pricing](https://sendcutsend.com/pricing/); [SendCutSend laser-cut steel](https://sendcutsend.com/materials/laser-cut-steel/); [SendCutSend blog on true cost](https://sendcutsend.com/blog/the-true-cost-of-custom-sheet-metal-parts/)
- OSH Cut positions itself as a comparable instant-quote laser service — [OSH Cut](https://www.oshcut.com/osh-cut-vs-send-cut-send)
- PCBWay CNC machining starts from $50; a user's aluminum machined block was quoted $257.69 and $334.08 with assembly, versus about $630 from a European shop — [Hackaday.io log](https://hackaday.io/project/190831/log/224322-a-pcbway-cnc-fabrication-costs-usd257-334); [PCBWay aluminum CNC](https://www.pcbway.com/rapid-prototyping/cnc-machining/metal/aluminum/)
- General CNC pricing guides: prototypes about $50-500+ per part; a single prototype might be $200 while 100 pieces drop to $25-40 each; Xometry is an instant-quote marketplace that adds a platform margin over a direct shop — [Xometry](https://www.xometry.com/capabilities/cnc-machining-service/); [Unionfab price guide](https://www.unionfab.com/blog/2024/12/cost-of-cnc); [XTJ on Xometry pricing](https://xtjcnc.com/blog/cnc-machining/xometry-cost/)
- The OpenChuck project deliberately avoids CNC and uses only drills, a compass/straightedge layout and optional belt sander, with a 13 mm reduced-shank drill and a hole saw on a bearing arbor substituting for a lathe when cutting the throat opening — [OpenChuck README](https://github.com/amasarac/OpenChuck)
- Practical Machinist has a thread cautioning shop owners about SendCutSend (content not retrievable) — [Practical Machinist](https://www.practicalmachinist.com/forum/threads/anyone-using-sendcutsend-com-if-youre-not-watch-out.379025/)

### Inferences
- Chuck adapter flanges: a 6 in K11/K12 plain-back chuck needs a plate with a 5.59 in bolt circle and a short register; a 1/4-3/8 in laser-cut steel disc with the bolt pattern and the spindle hole ($30-60 each at SendCutSend based on the 5 x 6 in example scaled up) gets within welding/boring range, but the register and face must still be trued on a lathe after it is welded to the spindle tube, so plan on one shop operation per spindle.
- Expect roughly $100-300 per spindle for a local shop to face and bore both ends of a DOM tube and skim the flange, judging from the $50-500 single-part CNC range; doing two spindles in one visit halves setup overhead.
- Tailstock base, bearing-block risers and the burner-post bracket are all flat parts suited to laser cutting from 1/4 in plate ($20-35 each at qty 1), with bends if needed; aluminum versions cost about the same from SendCutSend.

### Gaps
- No Xometry or JLCCNC quote for a specific lathe part (spindle or flange) was found; only generic ranges.
- SendCutSend's 1/4 in and 3/8 in steel per-part examples and its CNC-milling/tapping add-on prices were not retrievable (pricing page blocked; only the search-extracted examples above).
