# Glass Lathe Market and Pricing Survey (as of October 2026)

Method note for the report writer: direct page fetches were blocked by the network proxy for essentially every relevant domain (manufacturer sites, eBay, auction houses, forums, Wikipedia), so every finding below comes from search-engine result snippets/summaries of those pages, collected 2026-10-01. Listing prices from eBay/dealer pages are "asking" prices visible in search results at that date; the original listing dates are usually not visible. Where a price is a verified manufacturer list price it is labeled as such; where it is a forum anecdote it is labeled as such. Distinguish throughout between glassblowing (hot-working) lathes, which are the subject of this brief, and cold-working/engraving "glass lathes" (Covington, His Glassworks/Jim Moore, Spatzier/Merker style), which turn up in the same searches but are a different product.

## Key Question 1: Which companies make glass lathes today (and which historical makers still matter)?

### Takeaway
The premium tier is effectively two makers: Litton Engineering Laboratories (Grass Valley, CA, since 1932, ~52 models) and Herbert Arnold GmbH & Co. KG / Arnold Gruppe (Weilburg, Germany), which absorbed the UK's Heathway in 2005 and closed its Milton Keynes factory. Below them sit a US small-batch maker (SEAM Glass Lathes, California, assembling since 2005), Indian-built lathes sold through US dealers (Mountain Glass Arts, ABR Imagery), and a long tail of Chinese and Indian factories selling on Alibaba/Made-in-China/IndiaMART. Carlisle Machine Works and Bethlehem Apparatus/Burners make lathe burners and torches (Bethlehem made GL-50/GL-100 lathes historically) but no evidence was found that either currently builds lathes.

### Inferences
- The "premium" glassblowing-lathe market is a duopoly (Litton in the US, Arnold/Heathway in Europe), each decades old and building to order; everything else is a value tier built in India or China, sometimes tuned/branded by US dealers (Mountain Glass, ABR) or assembled domestically (SEAM).
- The ASGS classified "American Lathe model 5100" (88 mm bore) and the TalkGlass reference to an "S.E.M Industries 5100" are very likely the same SEAM/SEM product family; treat as probable, not confirmed.
- No evidence surfaced of GTT, Flow Glass, Pivotal, Carlisle or Litton marketing a purpose-built "mini" lathe for pipe makers beyond Litton's Art Lathe/Model F, Arnold's Precision Mini 1060 and the SEAM Mini; the "mini" segment is otherwise served by Chinese bench units.

### Gaps
- Could not open manufacturer pages directly, so complete spec tables (swing, bed length, speed ranges, motor HP, weights, synchronized vs. independent headstock/tailstock) for Litton's 52 models and Arnold's full catalog were not captured; only bore sizes and a few dimensions are available from snippets.
- No Japanese, Czech or Italian glassblowing-lathe manufacturers were identified; Japanese results returned only cold-glass processing machinery (Nakamura-Tome) and generic machine-tool makers. Cutting Edge Tech appears to be a Litton distributor in Japan rather than a maker.
- The Mountain Glass Lathe's bore and price were not visible in search snippets.
- "Bomar" returned only unrelated companies (a Toledo glass shop, a marine-hardware maker, a CNC shop); no Bomar glass lathe was found.

## Key Question 2: What do these lathes cost new?

### Takeaway
Verified list prices put a new Litton Model F at $16,990-$18,900 (Feb 2020) and a new Litton HSJ (114 mm bore) at $32,850-$40,650 for the bare lathe (Feb 2020); a new Arnold Precision Mini 1060 is $28,995 and the 1060/1080 are $39,995/$51,995 at ABR Imagery (2026 web prices). The value tier is roughly $3,600 (SEAM Mini) to $7,000-$9,000 (SEAM floor models, Indian 85 mm lathe at $7,500), and Chinese/Indian factory-direct units run from a few hundred dollars (tiny bench units of doubtful suitability) to about $3,000-$15,000 for 65-260 mm bore machines.

### Inferences
- Segmenting by size: "artist/bench" lathes (roughly 33-90 mm bore, 6-12" swing, 18-56" between chucks) cost $3,600-$9,000 from SEAM/Indian sources, $17,000-$19,000 from Litton (Model F, 2020 list), and $29,000 from Arnold (Mini 1060); "scientific/production" lathes (110 mm+ bore, 10' beds) cost $32,850-$51,995+ from Litton/Arnold in 2020-2026 list terms and "up to $150k" at the top of Litton's range per forum talk.
- The TalkGlass figures ($12-13.5k for a Litton art lathe; $8,750 for a SEM 5100) are consistent with Litton's $16,990 2020 list for the Model F if the forum posts date from roughly 2010-2015 and Litton has raised prices since; treat the forum numbers as historical.

## Key Question 6: Who buys glass lathes and how has demand changed?

### Takeaway
Core buyers remain scientific glassblowing shops (universities, national labs, contract shops such as Adams & Chittenden), fiber-optic/quartz/semiconductor fabricators (evidenced by the ex-Caltech preform lathe and Silicon Valley liquidation lots) and lamp/neon makers (Arnold's lamp-industry segment); since the 2010s the borosilicate functional-glass (pipe/dab-rig) boom has brought artists into the lathe market, which is what the SEAM, Mountain Glass and ABR value-tier offerings and Litton's Art Lathe target. Quantitative demand data was not found.
